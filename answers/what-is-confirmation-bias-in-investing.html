What is confirmation bias in investing?

Updated October 01, 2026 · AI How To Invest

Confirmation bias in investing requires understanding your goals, timeline, and risk tolerance before choosing investments.

Use Dollar-Cost Averaging

Investing a fixed amount regularly (weekly, biweekly, or monthly) removes the stress of trying to time the market. Historical data shows this approach reduces the risk of investing a large sum at a market peak.

Learn to Read Financial Statements

Understanding income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow statements helps you evaluate individual stocks more effectively. Focus on revenue growth, profit margins, and debt levels.

Understand Your Risk Tolerance

Your ideal portfolio allocation depends on your age, income stability, financial goals, and emotional comfort with market volatility. A 25-year-old can handle more stock exposure than a 60-year-old near retirement.

Keep Fees Low

A 1% difference in annual fees can cost you hundreds of thousands of dollars over a 30-year investment horizon. Always check expense ratios and prefer funds with costs below 0.20%.

Stay the Course During Downturns

The S&P 500 has recovered from every crash in history. Investors who panic sell during downturns lock in losses, while those who hold or buy more typically see strong recoveries within 1-3 years.

Build an Emergency Fund First

Before investing, set aside 3-6 months of living expenses in a high-yield savings account. This prevents you from being forced to sell investments at a loss during unexpected financial emergencies.
