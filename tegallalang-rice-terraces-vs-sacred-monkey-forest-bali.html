Tegallalang Rice Terraces or Sacred Monkey Forest First? Here's What Most Bali Tourists Get Wrong

Updated June 2026 · AttractionScout

Trying to decide between Tegallalang Rice Terraces and Sacred Monkey Forest in Bali? Both are top attractions, but they offer very different experiences. This comparison breaks down cost, time needed, best for whom, and our verdict to help you choose (or visit both).

Tegallalang Rice Terraces: What to Expect

Stunning green rice paddies carved into the hillside near Ubud. Tegallalang Rice Terraces is one of the most popular attractions in Bali and attracts millions of visitors each year. Tickets cost approximately $2 per adult.

Visitors typically spend 1.5 to 3 hours here. We recommend arriving early in the morning or late in the afternoon to avoid the largest crowds. Book skip-the-line tickets online in advance to save time.

Sacred Monkey Forest: What to Expect

Lush forest sanctuary home to 1,200 long-tailed macaques. Sacred Monkey Forest offers a different but equally rewarding experience in Bali. Tickets cost approximately $4 per adult.

Plan for 1.5 to 3 hours to fully appreciate what Sacred Monkey Forest has to offer. Like most popular Bali attractions, booking in advance is strongly recommended.

Price Comparison

Tegallalang Rice Terraces is the more affordable option, saving you approximately $2 per person.

Both attractions offer excellent value for the experience. If budget is a concern, consider purchasing a city pass or combo ticket that includes both at a discount.

Our Verdict: Which Should You Choose?

If you only have time for one, choose Tegallalang Rice Terraces if you prefer landmark experiences, or Sacred Monkey Forest if you lean toward landmark attractions. But honestly? If your schedule allows, visit both — they complement each other perfectly and together give you a well-rounded Bali experience.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best things to do in Bali?

Top experiences include visiting Uluwatu Temple at sunset (with Kecak fire dance), exploring Tegallalang Rice Terraces, surfing in Canggu, the Sacred Monkey Forest in Ubud, snorkeling in Nusa Penida, and watching the sunset at Tanah Lot temple.

Is Bali cheap for tourists?

Bali is very affordable. Local meals cost $2-5, a private villa can be $30-60/night, and motorbike rental is $5/day. Spa treatments, surfing lessons, and temple visits are all reasonably priced.

How many days do you need in Bali?

7-10 days is ideal to experience the diverse regions: 2-3 days in Ubud (culture/nature), 2-3 days in Seminyak/Canggu (beach/nightlife), 1-2 days in Uluwatu, and a day trip to Nusa Penida.
